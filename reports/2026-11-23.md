### Spending
Hyperscaler capex (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta combined) was $201.92B in 2026Q3, up 107.6% YoY. In 2026Q2 it was $165.05B, so spending grew 22.34% quarter over quarter. Capex intensity (capex as a share of revenue) is high. Microsoft spent $39.37B of capex against $92.28B of revenue in 2026Q3, which is 42.66%. Meta spent $33.94B against $64.6B, which is 52.54%. Capex is growing far faster than revenue at both companies. Microsoft's capex rose 103.0% against revenue growth of 18.8%. Meta's capex rose 80.3% against revenue growth of 26.1%.

### Suppliers
Growth is strongest closest to the GPU spend. Examples by layer:
- **Chips and memory:** Micron revenue rose 379.3% YoY in 2026Q3. Broadcom rose 85.5% in 2026Q2, and NVIDIA rose 105.8% in 2026Q2.
- **Servers and networking:** Super Micro rose 297.5% YoY in 2026Q3.
- **Power and cooling gear:** Vertiv rose 61.2% in 2026Q3.
- **Electricity:** Vistra rose 27.7% in 2026Q3, and Constellation rose 23.0% in 2026Q2.

Growth fades further down the chain. Equinix revenue rose 11.0% in 2026Q3. Quarters are not aligned across companies, and some growth rates come off small bases. NVIDIA's 2026Q3 revenue isn't public yet.

### Power
In Northern Virginia, overnight baseload rose 13.3% YoY in 2026Q3. Weather-adjusted growth was 8.7% over the last 12 months. In recent months it was higher: 12.9% in August, 13.2% in September and 12.0% in October. Weather added only 19 to 216 MW in those months, so heat doesn't explain the strength. The weather model fits well (R² 0.894). The zone has added 4,937 MW since 2019. The step-change screen found no jump above its 811 MW threshold, so the growth looks like gradual build-up rather than one large new load. Elsewhere, Ohio and central US grew 9.9% on a weather-adjusted 12-month basis. Dallas-Fort Worth grew 4.0%, although its actual 2026Q3 figure was 18.5%, so weather flattered it. These are single-station readings, and they include industry and electrification as well as data centers.

### In their own words
- NVIDIA attributes growth to Blackwell: "Revenue from Data Center computing grew 59% driven by demand for our Blackwell computing platform." Networking "grew 142% driven by the introduction and continued ramp of NVLink compute fabric for GB200 and GB300 systems" [NVDA 10-K filed 2026-02-25, MD&A ¶19]. These are fiscal-year figures, not the latest quarter.
- Dominion lists as a risk "the concentration of data centers primarily in Loudoun County, Virginia and the ability to obtain regulatory approvals, environmental and other permits to construct new facilities in a timely manner" [D 10-Q filed 2026-10-30, MD&A ¶8].

### What to watch
1. **NVIDIA's 2026Q3 report.** Capex growth was 107.6% in 2026Q3 and 87.0% in 2026Q2. NVIDIA's 2026Q2 growth was 105.9%. NVIDIA's revenue should keep pace with buyer spending. A clear shortfall would be a warning sign. I haven't run the backtest tools, so I'm not claiming that capex or grid data predicts NVIDIA's revenue.
2. **Capex running ahead of revenue.** Microsoft's capex growth was 5.48 times its revenue growth in 2026Q3. Meta's was 3.08 times. If cloud revenue growth slows, capex plans are the exposure.
3. **Grid momentum and permitting.** Watch whether weather-adjusted Northern Virginia growth holds in the 12.0% to 13.2% range of August to October or falls back toward the 6.0% seen in June. Also watch for any step change above 811 MW. Dominion's filing flags approvals as a constraint.
