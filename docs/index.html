follow-the-power ⚡

Tracking the AI boom through the electrical grid: NVIDIA's revenue, big tech's spending on data centers, and the power demand of the world's largest data-center hub, joined point-in-time and explained by a verified Claude analyst.

GitHub ↗

What is this?

AI runs on GPUs, and GPUs run on electricity. Every NVIDIA chip ends up plugged into a data center that draws power around the clock, and that shows up in public grid data. This project follows the money from big tech's budgets to NVIDIA's revenue to the power grid, using only official public data, and tests honestly what the grid can and can't tell you.

1

The money

Quarterly results from SEC filings: NVIDIA's revenue, and capital spending by Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta. Every number keeps the date it was first published.

2

The power

Hourly electricity demand in Northern Virginia (Dominion zone), home to the largest concentration of data centers on Earth. Data centers run 24/7, so they raise the overnight load.

The analyst

A Claude agent answers questions with these tools. Every number it writes is automatically checked against the data, and it's scored by replaying history so it can't peek at the future.

The power

Northern Virginia's overnight baseload

Median demand between 1 and 5 AM each month (MW), Dominion zone of PJM. Homes and offices are quiet at night; data centers aren't.

the floor of the line (the spring and fall lows, when weather matters least) keeps rising. That's new 24/7 load.

A flatter day: the data-center signature

Average load by hour as a % of the daily peak: 2019 vs. the last 12 months.

the recent line sits higher at night. Less difference between night and day means more always-on load, exactly what data centers add.

Monthly retail sales to commercial customers (data centers count as commercial), summed by quarter and indexed to each state's 2019 average.

Who leads whom

Correlation between growth rates at different time shifts. A bar at +k means the first series leads the second by k quarters. The shaded band is noise: bars inside it aren't meaningful (with only ~25 quarters, that band is wide).

NVIDIA's growth peaks a few quarters before capex growth (negative lags on the first pair), and capex growth peaks about one quarter before Virginia's overnight load (+1 on the second pair). Money, then chips, then power.

Can the grid forecast NVIDIA's revenue? An honest test

Each quarter, standing 7 days after it ends (before NVIDIA reports), three models predict NVIDIA's revenue growth using only data public that day. Lines show predictions vs. what NVIDIA later reported.

the grid model doesn't beat the naive "growth continues" guess. Grid data confirms the build-out after the money moves; it doesn't front-run NVIDIA. Reporting that plainly is the point.

Glossary

Hyperscaler capex
Capital expenditures of the giant cloud companies, mostly data centers, servers and the GPUs inside them.
Overnight baseload
The lowest, steadiest demand of the night. It rises when always-on loads like data centers are added.
Point-in-time data
Recording when each number was first published, so a backtest only uses information that existed on that date.
Year-over-year (YoY)
Growth vs. the same quarter a year earlier, which cancels out seasons.
Noise band
About 2/√n. Correlations smaller than this are indistinguishable from chance with n data points.
Naive forecast
"Next quarter grows as fast as last quarter." The baseline any model has to beat.
Number verifier
Code that checks every figure the agent writes against the numbers its tools returned. Unverifiable figures force a rewrite.
Skills
Reusable instruction packs the agent loads when a task needs them (e.g. how to write a variance note).
